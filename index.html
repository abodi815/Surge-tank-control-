Model equations

The equations used by the simulator are visible so students can connect the response to the process model.

Dynamic liquid balanceA · dh/dt = Fᵢₙ − FₒᵤₜIncompressible liquid; constant tank cross-sectional area.
Gravity outlet (Torricelli)Fₒᵤₜ = Cᵥ · √hThe outlet valve remains at its specified fixed opening in this model.
PI controllerFₚᵤₘₚ correction = Kp·e + Ki·∫e dte = setpoint − measured level; pump flow is limited to 0–20 m³/min.
Bottom gauge pressureP = ρ · g · hg = 9.81 m/s²; pressure is reported in kPa gauge.
  • Volumetric flow rates are used throughout; liquid density is constant during each run.
  • The tank is vertical with a constant cross-sectional area. The outlet discharges against atmospheric pressure.
  • In PI mode, the inlet pump is the manipulated variable. A positive pump correction increases inlet flow; a negative correction reduces it.
  • This is an ideal teaching model, not a detailed pump, valve, or mechanical design. The tank can exceed its stated height in the mathematical model; the visualization flags that condition.
Why doesn't density change the level response here? With volumetric inlet and outlet flow rates, density cancels from the incompressible liquid balance. Changing density changes ρgh (bottom pressure), but not dh/dt. Density would affect level dynamics if the specified flow were a mass flow rate (kg/min), because Q = ṁ/ρ.

Student exercise · Pump surge before a chemical reactor

Suggested classroom activity using the supplied solvent buffer tank case.

  1. Run the original case in Open-loop mode. Record the maximum level and compare it with the theoretical new steady level.
  2. Switch to PI feedback. Keep the +3 m³/min surge and setpoint at 4 m. Observe the pump correction and level response.
  3. Increase the tank area. Does the level move more slowly or more quickly after the same surge?
  4. Change the fluid from water to light oil. Which displayed variable changes, and which response stays the same?
  5. Try different Kp and Ti values. Find settings that give a smooth response without excessive oscillation.
Challenge questionWhy does the open-loop equilibrium become 7.5625 m after the surge, and why can the PI controller return the level toward 4 m without changing the fixed outlet valve?
